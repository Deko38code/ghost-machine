Website Design, Development and Hosting Agreement

This Agreement covers one client and one engagement. The Order Form in Schedule A sets the scope, price and timeline; everything else below applies to every engagement unless the Order Form explicitly changes it.

1. Parties and effective date

  1. This Agreement is made on between , of ("Provider"), and the client named in the Order Form ("Client").
  2. The person signing for Client represents that they are authorized to bind Client to this Agreement.

2. Services

  1. Scope. Provider will design, build, test and launch the website described in the Order Form, and will provide the hosting and care services selected there. Anything not written in the Order Form is out of scope.
  2. Out of scope by default. Unless the Order Form lists it: copywriting beyond the text Client supplies, photography and video production, logo and identity design, paid advertising, search-engine optimization retainers, social media management, third-party subscription costs, and data migration from systems Provider has not been given access to.
  3. AI-assisted tooling. Provider uses AI-assisted development tools under its own direction and review. Provider is responsible for the delivered result, but Client acknowledges that portions of the code may be machine-generated and human-reviewed rather than hand-typed line by line.
  4. Method. Provider chooses its own tools, frameworks, hosts and workflow. Provider may change them where it does not materially reduce what Client receives.

3. Client responsibilities

  1. Content. Client supplies the text, images, logos, product data, prices and claims for the site, and warrants that it owns or has permission to use all of it and that it is accurate and lawful.
  2. Decisions. Client names one contact person with authority to approve work, and responds to questions and approvals within five business days.
  3. Access. Client provides timely access to any domain registrar, hosting account, business profile or third-party account the work requires. Provider does not purchase accounts in Client's name without written instruction.
  4. Delays. A delay caused by Client extends the schedule by at least as long as the delay, and Provider may invoice work completed to that point.
  5. Compliance. Client is responsible for the legal and regulatory requirements of its own business, including licensing, advertising rules, consumer-protection rules and accessibility commitments beyond the build standard in the Order Form.

4. Fees and payment

  1. Price. Client pays the fees in the Order Form. Unless the Order Form says otherwise, half is due before build work starts and the rest before launch.
  2. Invoicing. Invoices are due within seven days. Late amounts carry interest at 1.5% per month or the highest rate the law allows, whichever is lower.
  3. Work stoppage. If an invoice is more than ten days overdue, Provider may pause work and suspend hosting after written notice, and may resume once the account is current. Suspension is not a waiver of anything owed.
  4. Disputes and chargebacks. Client will raise a billing dispute in writing before contacting its card issuer. Client agrees not to reverse a charge for work that was delivered as described. A chargeback for delivered work is a breach of this Agreement.
  5. Taxes. Fees exclude sales, use and similar taxes, which Client pays where applicable. Each party pays its own income taxes.
  6. Expenses. Third-party costs are billed at cost and only when approved in writing in advance.

5. Schedule, revisions and change orders

  1. Revisions. Two rounds of revisions within the agreed scope are included unless the Order Form says otherwise. Further rounds, or work outside the scope, are billed at Provider's hourly rate stated in the Order Form.
  2. Change orders. A change in scope is documented in writing with its effect on price and schedule, and takes effect when Client approves it. Approval resets the relevant dates.
  3. Acceptance. Client has five business days after a delivery notice to object in writing to a defect against the Order Form. Without a written objection in that window, the delivery is accepted.
  4. Launch. Provider launches the site when the build is accepted and all build fees are paid.

6. Launch, hosting and care

  1. Hosting term. Hosting and care run month to month under Schedule B and either party may end them with thirty days' written notice.
  2. Availability. Provider uses commercially reasonable efforts to keep the site online and reachable over HTTPS, and does not warrant uninterrupted or error-free operation.
  3. Exclusions from availability. Downtime caused by Client's own edits, third-party outages or policy changes, domain or DNS problems outside Provider's control, denial-of-service attacks, or events of force majeure does not count against availability.
  4. Backups. Provider keeps periodic backups but they are a convenience, not a data-recovery guarantee. Client keeps its own copy of all content and data it cannot afford to lose.
  5. Maintenance. Provider may take the site down briefly for maintenance and will avoid peak hours where practical.
  6. Security response. Provider may suspend or restrict the site to contain abuse, malware or a legal threat, and will tell Client why.

7. Domains and third-party services

  1. Domain ownership. A domain registered for Client is registered in Client's name and belongs to Client. Registration and renewal fees are Client's cost and Client's responsibility to keep current.
  2. Third parties. The site may depend on services such as Cloudflare, Stripe, Google or GitHub sign-in, and email delivery. Those services are governed by their own terms, and Provider is not responsible for their outages, pricing changes, rejections or account decisions.
  3. Credentials. Provider keeps API keys, OAuth secrets and payment credentials server-side and does not place them in page source, client-side code or public repositories. Client will not ask Provider to embed secrets in the browser.
  4. No platform guarantee. Provider does not guarantee approval or continued access on any third-party platform, including search engines, business profiles, app stores or advertising networks.

8. Intellectual property

  1. Transfer on payment. On receipt of all amounts due for the build, Provider assigns to Client the copyright in the custom design and code created for the site.
  2. Until payment. Until then Client has a limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable licence to review and use the work product, and ownership stays with Provider.
  3. Provider materials. Provider keeps ownership of its pre-existing tools, templates, libraries and know-how, and grants Client a perpetual, royalty-free licence to use any of them embedded in the delivered site so the site keeps working.
  4. Third-party assets. Fonts, stock images, icons and libraries remain under their own licences. Client pays any licence fee the Order Form attributes to Client.
  5. Portfolio. Unless Client opts out in writing, Provider may display the finished site, screenshots and a link in its portfolio and marketing.

9. Confidentiality

  1. Each party keeps the other's non-public business information confidential, uses it only to perform this Agreement, and protects it with reasonable care for three years after the engagement ends.
  2. This does not cover information that is public, independently developed, received from a third party without a duty, or required to be disclosed by law, provided the other party is notified where lawful.

10. Independent contractor

  1. Provider is an independent contractor. Nothing here creates employment, partnership, joint venture or agency, and neither party may bind the other.
  2. Provider supplies its own equipment and pays its own taxes, insurance and benefits. Client does not withhold taxes for Provider.

11. Warranties

  1. Provider warrants that the work will be performed in a workmanlike manner, will materially match the Order Form, and, to Provider's knowledge, will not infringe a third party's intellectual property.
  2. Provider will correct a defect reported within thirty days of launch at no charge if the defect is in the delivered work and not caused by Client's edits or a third-party change.
  3. No performance promises. Provider does not warrant search rankings, traffic, conversion rates, revenue, advertising results or the behaviour of third-party platforms or AI models.
  4. EXCEPT AS STATED ABOVE, THE SERVICES AND ANY HOSTING ARE PROVIDED "AS IS" AND "AS AVAILABLE", AND PROVIDER DISCLAIMS ALL OTHER WARRANTIES, EXPRESS OR IMPLIED, INCLUDING MERCHANTABILITY, FITNESS FOR A PARTICULAR PURPOSE AND NON-INFRINGEMENT.

12. Limitation of liability

  1. Except as stated in section 12.4, each party's total liability arising out of this Agreement is limited to the amount Client paid Provider under it in the twelve months before the claim, or the total build fee if that is less.
  2. Neither party is liable for indirect, incidental, special, punitive or consequential damages, or for lost profits, lost revenue, lost data or business interruption, even if advised they were possible.
  3. Client's sole remedy for a hosting or availability failure is to end the hosting service and receive a pro-rata refund of any prepaid hosting fees.
  4. Nothing in this section limits liability for gross negligence or wilful misconduct, breach of confidentiality, a party's indemnification obligations, or any liability that cannot be limited by law.

13. Indemnification

  1. By Client. Client will defend and indemnify Provider against claims, damages and reasonable legal costs arising from Client's content, Client's products or services, Client's own legal and regulatory compliance, or Client's misuse of the delivered site.
  2. By Provider. Provider will defend and indemnify Client against a third-party claim that the delivered custom work infringes a copyright or trademark, provided Client notifies Provider promptly, gives Provider control of the defence, and does not settle without consent. Provider's obligation is limited by section 12.
  3. Procedure. The indemnified party gives prompt notice, reasonable cooperation, and control of the defence to the indemnifying party.

14. Term, suspension and termination

  1. The build term runs until the site is accepted and launched. Hosting runs month to month under Schedule B.
  2. Either party may terminate for material breach if the breach is not cured within fifteen days of written notice. Provider may suspend service immediately for non-payment beyond the notice period in section 4.3, or for content that is unlawful or creates a security risk.
  3. On termination Client pays for work performed and expenses incurred through the termination date.
  4. On full payment Provider hands over the deliverables and any Client-owned accounts and credentials. Hosting stops at the end of the paid period and the site may go offline.
  5. Sections 4, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 15 and 16 survive termination.

15. Dispute resolution and governing law

  1. Talk first. The parties will attempt to resolve any dispute by written notice and thirty days of good-faith negotiation.
  2. Mediation, then arbitration. If negotiation fails, the dispute goes to mediation, and then to binding arbitration administered under the commercial rules of the American Arbitration Association, seated in County, . A single arbitrator decides, and judgment on the award may be entered in any court with jurisdiction.
  3. Carve-outs. Either party may bring an individual claim in small claims court, and either party may seek injunctive relief in court to protect intellectual property or confidential information.
  4. Class action waiver. Disputes are brought only individually, not as part of a class or representative action, to the extent the law allows.
  5. Fees. In arbitration the arbitrator may award the prevailing party its reasonable attorney fees and costs.
  6. Governing law. This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of , without regard to its conflict-of-laws rules.

16. General

  1. Entire agreement. This Agreement with its Schedules is the whole agreement and replaces any earlier proposal, quote or discussion on the same subject.
  2. Amendments. Changes must be in writing and signed or electronically accepted by both parties.
  3. Severability and waiver. If a provision is unenforceable it is cut back to the minimum needed and the rest stands. Not enforcing a term once is not a waiver.
  4. Assignment. Client may not assign this Agreement without Provider's written consent. Provider may assign it to a successor of the business.
  5. Notices. Written notices go to the addresses in the Order Form, or by email with confirmation of receipt. Notice to Provider: .
  6. Force majeure. Neither party is liable for a delay caused by events beyond its reasonable control, provided it notifies the other and resumes promptly.
  7. Counterparts and e-signature. This Agreement may be signed in counterparts and by electronic signature, which is binding under the federal E-SIGN Act and applicable state law.
  8. Independent counsel. Each party has had the chance to consult its own lawyer and signs voluntarily.

What these documents do, and what they don't

These are provider-side drafts, written to be reviewed and adjusted by a lawyer licensed in your state. They are a starting point that reflects how you actually work, not a substitute for advice.

  • 01Have a lawyer read them once. Arbitration clauses, late-fee rates, liability caps and the release in Document 2 are the parts most often tested. A one-hour review is cheap next to a dispute.
  • 02Sign as an entity, not as yourself. An LLC or corporation is what keeps a business claim off your personal assets. If you sign personally, the liability cap protects the business, not you.
  • 03Keep the paper trail. Signed Order Forms, invoices, payment records and change orders are what make these clauses usable. Document 2's payment record section is only worth something if you actually keep the record.
  • 04Match the offer to the paper. The Order Form must state the real price and features. A contract that promises something the site does not do is worse than no contract.
  • 05Insurance. A technology errors-and-omissions policy covers the claims these contracts limit but do not eliminate. A liability cap does not pay a lawyer.
  • 06Late fees have a ceiling. Usury limits cap what 1.5% per month can mean in some states. The clause already says "or the highest rate the law allows"; confirm the number for your state.
  • 07Chargeback clauses do not override card rules. They give you a contractual claim if a client reverses a delivered charge; the card network still decides the reversal itself.
  • 08Deliver what the contract says. Handover on final payment, the licence that keeps embedded tools working, and the thirty-day defect window are promises. Keep them and the rest of the document holds up.

Not legal advice. These drafts were prepared for review, not for signature as-is. Laws differ by state and change over time, and how a clause is enforced depends on the facts of a dispute.
